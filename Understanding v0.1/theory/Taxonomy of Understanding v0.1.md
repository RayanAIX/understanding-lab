# Taxonomy of Understanding

### Understanding Lab — Theory Series
### Version: 0.1 (Working Theory)
### Status: Provisional
### Date: August 2026

# Purpose of This Document

The Understanding Lab investigates one central question:

> **What does it mean to truly understand something?**

The **Properties of Understanding** document identified candidate characteristics that may be associated with genuine understanding.

The **Non-Examples of Understanding** document identified ways in which successful performance can occur without establishing understanding.

This document addresses a different question:

> **If understanding is not a single, binary phenomenon, what different forms can it take?**

A useful theory must distinguish different objects, contents, modes, depths, and functional manifestations of understanding without turning every distinction into a separate definition.

The purpose of this taxonomy is therefore to provide a **structured map of the space of understanding**.

It is not intended to claim that the categories below are natural kinds, mutually exclusive psychological states, or a final ontology.

They are a **working taxonomy** designed to:

- organize the literature,
- prevent different senses of "understanding" from being conflated,
- generate research questions,
- guide operationalization,
- support the design of the Same Mind Project,
- constrain HCMS v2,
- and provide a vocabulary for future empirical work.

The taxonomy should be revised when evidence shows that categories should be merged, separated, renamed, or rejected.

---

# Central Principle

> **Understanding should not be treated as one undifferentiated capacity.**

In ordinary language, we say that someone:

- understands that something is true,
- understands why it happened,
- understands how a system works,
- understands a subject,
- understands another person,
- understands a language,
- understands how to perform a task.

These uses may overlap, but they need not describe exactly the same cognitive achievement.

Philosophical work distinguishes, among other possibilities, **propositional understanding** from **objectual understanding**, while broader discussions identify forms such as understanding how and understanding why.

Educational research has also distinguished **instrumental** and **relational** understanding, particularly in mathematics, with relational understanding emphasizing connections, reasons, and adaptability rather than merely following rules.

The Understanding Lab therefore adopts a critical distinction:

> **A taxonomy describes different forms or dimensions of understanding; it does not automatically define which of them are necessary for understanding in general.**

---

# Why a Taxonomy Is Necessary

Without a taxonomy, several conceptual errors become likely.

## Error 01 — Treating all understanding as propositional

Someone may understand:

> "Why the bridge collapsed."

That is different from understanding:

> "Structural engineering."

The first can be expressed as a specific proposition or explanation.

The second concerns a broader body of interconnected information.

The literature explicitly distinguishes these object-level and proposition-level uses.

---

## Error 02 — Treating procedural competence as identical to conceptual grasp

A person may know how to execute a procedure while having a weak grasp of its underlying structure.

Skemp's influential distinction between instrumental and relational understanding captures this difference: instrumental understanding concerns rules and procedures, whereas relational understanding concerns knowing what to do **and why**, including connections among concepts.

The Lab should not simply declare one "real" and the other "fake."

Instead, it should investigate:

> **What kind of understanding does each represent, what are its capabilities, and where are its limits?**

---

## Error 03 — Treating depth as type

A shallow understanding of a concept and a deep understanding of the same concept may belong to the same basic type while differing in degree.

Therefore:

> **Type and degree should be kept conceptually separate.**

---

## Error 04 — Treating dimensions as categories

Explanation, prediction, transfer, coherence, and calibration may be properties or indicators of understanding.

They are not necessarily different *types* of understanding.

For example:

> "Predictive understanding"

may be useful as a descriptive phrase, but it should not automatically become a new category in the taxonomy.

Otherwise the taxonomy simply duplicates the Properties document.

---

# Taxonomic Design Principle

The current taxonomy separates understanding along **four major axes**:

```text
                         UNDERSTANDING
                               │
       ┌───────────────────────┼────────────────────────┐
       │                       │                        │
     OBJECT                  MODE                    SCOPE
       │                       │                        │
 What is understood?     What kind of grasp?     How much is grasped?
       │                       │                        │
 proposition            declarative              local
 object/topic           explanatory              relational
 system/process         procedural               systemic
 person/language        model-based              domain-level
       │                       │
       └───────────────────────┴────────────────────────┐
                                                        │
                                                      DEGREE
                                                        │
                                              How robust is the grasp?
                                                        │
                                      shallow → functional → deep → expert
```

These axes are **not mutually exclusive categories**.

They are dimensions that can combine.

For example:

> A person may have **deep explanatory understanding of a causal mechanism within a specific scientific domain**, while having only shallow understanding of the wider field.

That is more informative than assigning the person a single label such as "deep understanding."

---

# Axis I — Object of Understanding

The first question is:

> **What is the person trying to understand?**

## Type 01 — Propositional Understanding

Propositional understanding concerns understanding that something is the case, or understanding a particular fact, claim, or proposition.

Examples:

- understanding that water expands when it freezes,
- understanding that a particular event occurred,
- understanding that a mathematical statement follows from given premises.

### Typical evidence

- accurate articulation,
- justification,
- ability to connect the proposition to relevant reasons,
- ability to identify implications or exceptions.

### Critical question

> Is the proposition understood in isolation, or as part of a larger structure?

A proposition may be correctly understood while remaining poorly integrated into a broader conceptual system.

---

## Type 02 — Explanatory Understanding

Explanatory understanding concerns grasping:

> **why** something is the case,

or:

> **how** a phenomenon comes about.

Examples:

- understanding why seasons occur,
- understanding how a disease spreads,
- understanding why a market responds to a particular change.

### Typical evidence

- causal explanation,
- mechanism identification,
- counterfactual reasoning,
- ability to anticipate consequences.

### Critical question

> Can the explanation support reasoning beyond the explanation itself?

An explanation that cannot generate consequences may be memorized rather than deeply understood.

---

## Type 03 — Procedural Understanding

Procedural understanding concerns knowing:

> **how to do something.**

Examples:

- how to solve a type of equation,
- how to operate an instrument,
- how to execute a laboratory procedure,
- how to train a model.

### Typical evidence

- successful execution,
- sequencing of actions,
- error correction,
- appropriate procedure selection.

### Critical question

> Does the person understand when, why, and under what conditions the procedure works?

This distinguishes procedural competence from broader conceptual or relational grasp.

---

## Type 04 — Objectual Understanding

Objectual understanding concerns understanding:

> **a subject, topic, body of information, or domain.**

Examples:

- understanding thermodynamics,
- understanding economics,
- understanding a political system,
- understanding ecology.

Objectual understanding is more holistic than propositional understanding.

### Typical evidence

- integration of multiple concepts,
- ability to navigate relationships,
- explanation of central mechanisms,
- application across cases,
- identification of important distinctions.

### Critical question

> How much of the relevant structure must be grasped before objectual understanding exists?

This is a central problem for measurement.

---

## Type 05 — Structural or Systemic Understanding

A person may understand not merely a subject, but the **organization and dependencies within a system**.

Examples:

- understanding how an ecosystem responds to environmental change,
- understanding how components of a computer system interact,
- understanding how incentives shape a market,
- understanding how an organization behaves as a system.

### Typical evidence

- relationship mapping,
- system-level prediction,
- identifying dependencies,
- reasoning about interventions.

### Critical question

> Can the person reason about changes to one part of the system through their consequences for other parts?

---

## Type 06 — Interpersonal Understanding

Understanding can also concern:

> **another person's beliefs, intentions, emotions, reasons, or perspective.**

Examples:

- understanding why someone acted as they did,
- understanding another person's perspective,
- understanding the social dynamics of a group.

This is included cautiously.

The Lab is not claiming that interpersonal understanding has exactly the same cognitive structure as scientific or mathematical understanding.

Rather:

> The ordinary concept of understanding extends beyond subject-matter comprehension.

### Research question

Can the general framework account for understanding people without reducing interpersonal understanding to factual knowledge?

---

# Axis II — Mode of Understanding

The second question is:

> **In what mode is the subject grasped?**

Different modes can coexist.

## Mode 01 — Declarative Understanding

The person can articulate:

- what something is,
- what properties it has,
- what relationships hold.

Declarative understanding is often easier to assess linguistically.

But language alone remains vulnerable to the confounds identified in the Non-Examples document.

---

## Mode 02 — Explanatory Understanding

The person can generate or evaluate accounts of:

- why,
- how,
- under what conditions.

This overlaps with explanatory understanding as an object type, but here it is treated as a **mode** through which a subject is understood.

The distinction matters because someone can possess an explanatory grasp of a broader subject without understanding every proposition within it.

---

## Mode 03 — Procedural Understanding

The person can act appropriately within the relevant system.

This includes:

- selecting a method,
- executing it,
- monitoring its outcome,
- correcting errors.

Procedural and conceptual understanding should not be treated as simple opposites. Conceptual knowledge can guide procedure selection, while procedural activity can support conceptual development.

---

## Mode 04 — Model-Based Understanding

The person can construct, manipulate, or use a representation of a system.

The representation might be:

- verbal,
- visual,
- mathematical,
- spatial,
- computational,
- analogical,
- causal.

A model is not necessarily a literal copy of reality.

Scientific understanding can involve idealizations and models that are useful despite not representing every feature of the world.

---

## Mode 05 — Relational Understanding

Relational understanding concerns grasping:

- connections,
- dependencies,
- patterns,
- reasons,
- relationships among concepts.

Skemp's relational understanding is a particularly influential example: the learner understands both what to do and why, while building a conceptual structure that can adapt to new tasks.

This mode is particularly important for the Understanding Lab because it connects directly to:

- structural representation,
- coherence,
- transfer,
- adaptation,
- prediction.

---

## Mode 06 — Generative Understanding

A person demonstrates understanding by being able to **produce new consequences from what is understood**.

Examples:

- generating a new example,
- deriving a consequence,
- constructing a new explanation,
- solving a novel problem,
- producing a prediction.

This is not proposed as a universally accepted philosophical category.

It is a **working research category** motivated by the Lab's interest in distinguishing understanding from recognition and reproduction.

### Central question

> Does genuine understanding increase the ability to generate relevant outputs under conditions where memorized outputs are unavailable?

---

## Mode 07 — Metacognitive Understanding

A person may understand both the subject and aspects of their own epistemic position within it.

This includes awareness of:

- uncertainty,
- limitations,
- assumptions,
- errors,
- confidence,
- what remains unknown.

This connects to the Property of Calibration.

However:

> **Calibration is not itself understanding.**

It is a possible dimension or supporting capacity.

---

# Axis III — Scope of Understanding

The third question is:

> **How much of the relevant conceptual territory is understood?**

## Scope 01 — Local Understanding

Understanding is restricted to a particular proposition, mechanism, procedure, or small conceptual region.

Example:

> Understanding why a particular algorithm has a certain computational complexity.

A person may understand this deeply without understanding the entire field of computer science.

---

## Scope 02 — Relational Understanding

The learner understands a concept **in relation to neighboring concepts**.

Example:

Understanding opportunity cost not merely as a definition, but in relation to:

- scarcity,
- choice,
- incentives,
- trade-offs,
- resource allocation.

---

## Scope 03 — Systemic Understanding

The person can understand interactions among many components of a system.

Examples:

- ecological systems,
- economies,
- organizations,
- biological systems,
- complex software architectures.

Systemic understanding requires more than accumulating facts.

It requires grasping dependencies and their consequences.

---

## Scope 04 — Domain Understanding

The person has a broad, structured grasp of a subject area.

Examples:

- physics,
- medicine,
- mathematics,
- history,
- machine learning.

Domain understanding is likely composed of many local and relational understandings rather than being a single indivisible state.

---

## Scope 05 — Cross-Domain Understanding

A person recognizes structures or principles that remain useful across different domains.

Examples:

- recognizing feedback loops in biology and economics,
- recognizing optimization principles in machine learning and operations research,
- recognizing causal reasoning patterns across sciences.

This is an ambitious category.

The Lab should **not assume that cross-domain transfer automatically demonstrates deeper understanding**.

It should remain an empirical question.

---

# Axis IV — Degree and Robustness

The fourth question is:

> **How strong, stable, flexible, and complete is the understanding?**

Philosophical work explicitly discusses understanding as coming in degrees.

The following levels are provisional.

## Level 0 — No Demonstrable Understanding

There is insufficient evidence of a relevant grasp.

This does **not** necessarily mean:

> the person has no understanding internally.

It means:

> the available evidence does not support a reliable inference of understanding.

This distinction is essential for measurement.

---

## Level 1 — Recognition

The person can recognize familiar information or identify an answer when presented with cues.

Recognition is useful but weak evidence because it is vulnerable to familiarity and cue dependence.

---

## Level 2 — Reproduction

The person can reproduce relevant information, explanations, procedures, or examples.

This may coexist with genuine understanding, but reproduction alone does not establish it.

---

## Level 3 — Functional Understanding

The person can use the relevant knowledge or procedure successfully in expected contexts.

This is stronger than recognition or reproduction but remains vulnerable to overfitting and familiarity.

---

## Level 4 — Relational Understanding

The person can connect concepts, explain relationships, identify reasons, and integrate information into a coherent structure.

---

## Level 5 — Generative Understanding

The person can use the underlying structure to produce:

- novel explanations,
- predictions,
- applications,
- examples,
- counterexamples,
- solutions.

---

## Level 6 — Adaptive Understanding

The person can maintain useful performance when:

- wording changes,
- contexts change,
- assumptions change,
- evidence changes,
- the original strategy fails.

They can revise their model rather than merely repeating the old one.

---

## Level 7 — Deep / Robust Understanding

The person demonstrates a stable, coherent, flexible grasp across:

- multiple contexts,
- novel problems,
- altered assumptions,
- explanatory tasks,
- predictive tasks,
- transfer tasks,
- revision tasks.

This is a target construct for future empirical investigation, not a final definition.

---

# Important Warning: These Levels Are Not Yet a Validated Scale

The sequence above is useful as a conceptual scaffold:

```text
Recognition
     ↓
Reproduction
     ↓
Functional Use
     ↓
Relational Grasp
     ↓
Generation
     ↓
Adaptation
     ↓
Robustness
```

But the Understanding Lab does **not** currently claim that understanding develops linearly through these seven levels.

A person may:

- possess strong procedural competence but weak explanatory understanding,
- have excellent relational understanding but poor verbal expression,
- understand a mechanism deeply but lack broad domain knowledge,
- generate correct predictions while holding a partially false model,
- understand something deeply while remaining uncertain about it.

Therefore the levels should not yet be used as a universal scoring rubric.

They are hypotheses about **patterns of capability**, not established developmental stages.

---

# Critical Distinction: Type ≠ Level ≠ Property ≠ Indicator

One of the most important conceptual safeguards for the Lab is to keep four categories separate.

## Type

> **What kind of understanding is this?**

Examples:

- propositional,
- procedural,
- objectual,
- explanatory,
- interpersonal.

## Level / Degree

> **How developed or robust is it?**

Examples:

- functional,
- relational,
- generative,
- adaptive.

## Property

> **What characteristic may understanding involve?**

Examples:

- coherence,
- structural representation,
- transfer,
- prediction,
- adaptation.

## Indicator

> **What observable evidence might allow us to infer it?**

Examples:

- explanation task,
- transfer task,
- prediction task,
- confidence rating,
- model-revision task.

These should not be collapsed.

For example:

> Transfer is not necessarily a type of understanding.

It may be a **property** or an **indicator** of some forms of understanding.

Likewise:

> Explanation is not automatically a type.

It can be a mode through which understanding is expressed and an observable indicator of underlying cognitive structure.

This distinction prevents the taxonomy from becoming conceptually circular.

# A Multi-Axial Model

The current taxonomy is best represented not as a tree, but as a **multi-dimensional space**.

```text
                         UNDERSTANDING
                              │
              ┌───────────────┼───────────────┐
              │               │               │
           OBJECT            MODE           SCOPE
              │               │               │
        proposition      declarative        local
        mechanism        explanatory        relational
        procedure        procedural         systemic
        topic/domain     model-based        domain
        system           relational          cross-domain
        person           generative
                         metacognitive
              │               │               │
              └───────────────┼───────────────┘
                              │
                            DEGREE
                              │
             recognition → reproduction → function
                              ↓
                    relational → generative
                              ↓
                       adaptive → robust
```

A real instance of understanding occupies a position across several dimensions.

For example:

> **A student may have strong procedural understanding of solving quadratic equations, moderate relational understanding of the underlying algebraic structure, local scope, and weak transfer to unfamiliar contexts.**

That description is far more informative than:

> "The student understands quadratics."

---

# Understanding Is Likely Compositional

The taxonomy suggests that what we casually call "understanding" may actually involve multiple partially separable capacities.

For example:

```text
                    "Understanding Gravity"
                              │
          ┌───────────────────┼───────────────────┐
          │                   │                   │
       WHAT?                WHY?                HOW?
          │                   │                   │
    propositions         mechanisms          procedures
          │                   │                   │
          └───────────────────┼───────────────────┘
                              │
                       STRUCTURAL MODEL
                              │
                ┌─────────────┼─────────────┐
                │             │             │
             predict       transfer       adapt
                │             │             │
                └─────────────┼─────────────┘
                              │
                     broader understanding
```

This does not mean that every form must contain every component.

Rather:

> **Different tasks may reveal different slices of a person's understanding.**

This is one reason why the Lab should avoid designing HCMS as a single-score test too early.

---

# Partial Understanding

A major implication of the taxonomy is that understanding can be **asymmetric**.

Someone may understand:

- the mechanism but not the history,
- the procedure but not the reason,
- the local case but not the general principle,
- the model but not its limitations,
- the concept but not its applications.

Therefore:

> **Partial understanding is not an anomaly. It may be the normal condition of human understanding.**

This supports the earlier property of **degree** while adding another idea:

> Understanding may vary not only in **amount**, but in **structure**.

Two people can have the same overall performance score while possessing very different profiles of understanding.

This possibility is central to the future measurement problem.

---

# Understanding Profiles

Instead of representing understanding as:

```text
Understanding = 73%
```

the Lab may eventually represent it as a profile:

```text
Concept
│
├── Propositional grasp      ████████░░
├── Explanatory grasp        █████████░
├── Procedural competence    ██████████
├── Structural coherence     ███████░░░
├── Prediction               ████████░░
├── Transfer                 █████░░░░░
├── Adaptation               ██████░░░░
└── Calibration              ████████░░
```

This is **not** a proposed scoring system yet.

It is a conceptual possibility arising from the taxonomy.

The important idea is:

> **A single scalar score may conceal the structure of understanding.**

HCMS v2 should therefore investigate whether multidimensional profiles provide more information than a single composite score.

# Taxonomy vs. Non-Examples

The two frameworks serve different functions.

```text
TAXONOMY
   │
   ↓
What kinds of understanding might exist?
   │
   ↓
NON-EXAMPLES
   │
   ↓
What can imitate evidence of understanding?
   │
   ↓
OPERATIONAL INDICATORS
   │
   ↓
What observations discriminate between them?
```

For example:

### Candidate form

Procedural understanding

### Possible confound

Pattern matching or memorized procedure

### Discriminative question

Can the person:

- select the correct procedure when the surface form changes?
- explain why it works?
- identify when it does not apply?
- adapt it under altered conditions?

This is the bridge from taxonomy to measurement.

---

# Taxonomy vs. Properties

The distinction is equally important.

### Taxonomy asks:

> **What form does the understanding take?**

### Properties ask:

> **What characteristics might genuine understanding exhibit?**

For example:

A person may have:

> **objectual + relational + systemic understanding**

that exhibits:

> **coherence + structural representation + predictive capacity + transfer.**

The first describes the **kind and scope of grasp**.

The second describes **candidate characteristics of that grasp**.

---

# Taxonomy vs. Indicators

An indicator is not the construct itself.

For example:

```text
Transfer Task
     ↓
Observable performance
     ↓
Evidence
     ↓
Inference about generalization
     ↓
Possible evidence of understanding
```

The task does not directly "measure understanding."

It provides evidence from which an inference may be made.

This distinction should remain explicit throughout HCMS development.

---

# The Hard Cases

A useful taxonomy should not only describe easy cases.

It must survive difficult ones.

## Case 01 — Deep Procedural Understanding

A surgeon may perform a procedure with extraordinary competence while being unable to provide a complete verbal explanation of every underlying mechanism.

Question:

> Is verbal explanation necessary for understanding?

The taxonomy leaves this open.

---

## Case 02 — Understanding With False Beliefs

A scientist may use an idealized model that is technically false but structurally useful.

Question:

> Can genuine understanding exist through a partially false model?

The taxonomy therefore does not define truth as a simple binary gate.

---

## Case 03 — Understanding Without Transfer

A person may demonstrate strong understanding in a tightly defined domain but fail to recognize its application in a distant context.

Question:

> Is this a failure of understanding or a failure of transfer?

The taxonomy keeps these concepts separate.

---

## Case 04 — Understanding Without Confidence

An expert may understand a complex phenomenon while being highly uncertain because the evidence itself is uncertain.

Therefore:

> Low confidence cannot automatically imply low understanding.

Calibration is a supporting dimension, not a gate.

---

## Case 05 — Understanding Without Language

A person may demonstrate sophisticated understanding through:

- action,
- visual modeling,
- spatial reasoning,
- manipulation,
- prediction.

This motivates the inclusion of nonverbal and model-based modes.

---

## Case 06 — AI Systems

An AI system may:

- answer correctly,
- explain correctly,
- transfer to novel examples,
- generate predictions,
- revise answers after feedback.

Yet these behaviors do not by themselves settle whether the system possesses understanding in the same sense as a human.

This is a major future research problem.

The taxonomy should therefore be usable for **behavioral characterization** without prematurely deciding the metaphysical status of AI understanding.

---

# A Stronger Research Question Emerges

The taxonomy reveals that the question:

> "Does this person understand X?"

may be too coarse.

A more precise question is:

> **"What kind of understanding of X does this person possess, to what degree, across which contexts, and with what observable capabilities?"**

This changes the research target from:

```text
UNDERSTANDS / DOES NOT UNDERSTAND
```

to:

```text
UNDERSTANDING PROFILE
        │
        ├── object
        ├── mode
        ├── scope
        ├── degree
        └── robustness
```

This may become a foundational design principle for HCMS.

---

# Candidate Research Hypotheses

## H1 — Understanding Is Multidimensional

Individuals with similar correctness scores will differ systematically in their profiles across explanation, procedure, structural grasp, transfer, prediction, and adaptation.

## H2 — Understanding Types Are Partially Dissociable

Strong performance in one mode of understanding will not guarantee equally strong performance in other modes.

For example:

> procedural competence ≠ explanatory competence.

## H3 — Relational Structure Predicts Flexibility

Individuals with stronger relational organization will show greater adaptability to novel tasks than individuals whose performance depends primarily on isolated procedures.

## H4 — Degree Is Not Equivalent to Breadth

A person can possess very deep local understanding without broad domain understanding.

Therefore breadth and depth should be measured separately.

## H5 — Robust Understanding Is Profiled, Not Captured by One Behavior

No single task will reliably distinguish all forms of understanding.

A combination of observations across contexts may provide stronger evidence.

## H6 — Taxonomic Profile May Predict Future Performance Better Than Correctness Alone

A multidimensional understanding profile may predict performance on novel tasks better than conventional correctness scores.

This is a particularly important empirical hypothesis for HCMS.

---

# Implications for Same Mind

The Same Mind Project should not merely ask:

> "Do people who answer the same question correctly understand it differently?"

It should ask a more precise question:

> **"When people produce comparable successful answers, do they exhibit different profiles of understanding across object, mode, scope, and robustness?"**

For a concept such as gravity, participants could potentially be examined through:

- proposition questions,
- explanatory questions,
- procedural/application tasks,
- model construction,
- prediction,
- transfer,
- counterexample generation,
- adaptation after new information,
- confidence and calibration.

The experiment should not assume that all these tasks are valid indicators.

Rather:

> **The taxonomy provides candidate dimensions whose discriminative value can be tested.**

This makes Same Mind a test of the taxonomy itself.

The taxonomy suggests a major architectural shift.

### HCMS v1

Primarily asks:

> How well did the learner perform?

### HCMS v2 may eventually ask:

> What kind of understanding does the learner appear to have, how robust is it, and which alternative explanations remain plausible?

# What the Taxonomy Does Not Claim

The Understanding Lab does **not** currently claim that:

- these are the only types of understanding,
- every person possesses every type,
- every type is equally important,
- the categories are psychologically independent,
- the categories are mutually exclusive,
- the levels form a fixed developmental sequence,
- transfer is always necessary,
- explanation is always necessary,
- prediction is always necessary,
- procedural understanding is inferior,
- relational understanding is always deeper,
- verbal performance is a reliable proxy for cognition,
- understanding requires a particular representation,
- AI systems possess or lack understanding.

These questions remain open.

# A More Fundamental Possibility

The taxonomy may eventually reveal that "understanding" is not best modeled as a collection of discrete types at all.

There are at least three competing possibilities:

### Model A — Categorical

Understanding consists of distinct types.

```text
Propositional
Procedural
Explanatory
Objectual
...
```

### Model B — Dimensional

Understanding is one multidimensional construct.

```text
                 Understanding
        ┌──────────┬──────────┬──────────┐
      structure  flexibility  scope   ...
```

### Model C — Profile / State-Space

Understanding is a configuration across multiple dimensions that changes with:

- subject,
- context,
- task,
- time,
- representation,
- prior knowledge.

```text
                 Understanding State
                         │
          ┌──────────────┼──────────────┐
        object          mode           scope
          │              │               │
        depth         flexibility      coherence
          │              │               │
          └──────────────┼───────────────┘
                         ↓
                  capability profile
```

The Understanding Lab should **not decide this theoretically in advance**.

One of the eventual goals of empirical research should be to determine which structure best explains the data.

This is potentially more important than refining the taxonomy indefinitely.

# Pressure Tests

The taxonomy should eventually survive the following tests.

## PT-001 — Distinctness

Do the proposed categories correspond to empirically distinguishable patterns?

## PT-002 — Overlap

Can one person possess multiple forms simultaneously?

If so, can the framework represent this without contradiction?

## PT-003 — Necessity

Is any proposed category actually necessary for understanding?

## PT-004 — Sufficiency

Can a person possess one category without possessing broader understanding?

## PT-005 — Degree

Can the framework represent partial and graded understanding?

## PT-006 — Robustness

Can it distinguish understanding that survives novel conditions from understanding that is context-bound?

## PT-007 — False Models

Can it represent genuine understanding supported by idealized or partially false models?

## PT-008 — Nonverbal Understanding

Can it represent understanding expressed through action, visualization, or simulation rather than language?

## PT-009 — Human Variation

Can it distinguish understanding from differences in:

- language ability,
- memory,
- processing speed,
- anxiety,
- communication skill,
- accessibility?

## PT-010 — AI Generality

Can the framework characterize AI behavior without assuming in advance that behavioral competence is equivalent to human understanding?

## PT-011 — Predictive Utility

Does the taxonomy improve prediction of performance on future tasks?

## PT-012 — Measurement Utility

Does using the taxonomy actually improve the ability to discriminate understanding from the non-examples identified in the previous document?

> **A beautiful taxonomy that does not improve inference is not a successful scientific taxonomy.**

# Current Position of the Understanding Lab

The Understanding Lab currently treats understanding as a **potentially multidimensional, graded, context-sensitive cognitive achievement** rather than a binary possession of information.

The working taxonomy distinguishes:

1. **Object** — what is understood.
2. **Mode** — how it is understood or expressed.
3. **Scope** — how much of the relevant structure is grasped.
4. **Degree** — how robust and flexible the understanding is.

The Lab does not yet claim that these four dimensions constitute the true structure of understanding.

Instead:

> **They provide a testable coordinate system for describing candidate forms of understanding.**

The central scientific question now becomes:

> **Do these dimensions correspond to real, measurable differences in human cognitive performance?**

---

# Research Note

The most important contribution of this document is not the number of categories.

It is the distinction between:

> **"understanding" as a single label**

and

> **the structure of the understanding a person actually possesses.**

A person may know *what* without knowing *why*.

They may know *how* without knowing *when*.

They may understand a mechanism without understanding the wider system.

They may understand locally but fail to transfer globally.

They may perform correctly while possessing a fragile model.

They may possess a deep model while being unable to articulate every part of it.

Therefore:

> **The question "Does this person understand?" may be scientifically under-specified.**

A better question is:

> **What structure of understanding does the person possess, how robust is it, where does it generalize, and what alternative explanations remain consistent with the observed performance?**

If future evidence supports this view, the taxonomy could become one of the conceptual foundations of the Understanding Lab's measurement program.

But the Lab should remain willing to discard it.

The goal is not to build a beautiful taxonomy.

The goal is to discover whether the taxonomy corresponds to reality.

---

# Status

This document represents a **provisional taxonomy of candidate forms and dimensions of understanding**.

It is not a validated psychological scale.

It is not a claim that all listed categories are independent.

It is not a final ontology of understanding.

Its purpose is to provide a structured hypothesis space from which measurable predictions can be generated.

Future evidence may:

- merge categories,
- split categories,
- eliminate categories,
- reveal missing dimensions,
- show that some dimensions are continuous rather than categorical,
- or show that the entire taxonomy is the wrong model.

That possibility is not a weakness.

It is part of the research design.
